Martian Surface Micro-Abrasion & Capacitive State Observer Formulation

Rigorous mathematical models mapping saltating regolith kinetic energy fluxes to anti-reflective coating dielectric thinning and high-frequency permittivity degradation.

1. Kinetic Particle Scouring & Abrasive Wear Equation

Martian saltating regolith particles ($SiO_2$, $Fe_2O_3$, basalts) driven by boundary-layer shear winds deliver continuous impact energy to solar cover-glass. The volumetric removal rate $\dot{V}_{\text{erosion}}$ follows a modified Bitter-Finnie ductile-brittle impact erosion law:

$$\dot{V}_{\text{erosion}} = \frac{1}{2 \sigma_{\text{yield}} H_m} \sum_{i=1}^{N_p} m_i v_i^2 \cos^2(\alpha_i) \cdot f(\theta_i) + \frac{m_i (v_i \sin(\alpha_i) - v_t)^2}{2 \epsilon_{\text{brittle}}}$$

Where $H_m$ is the Mohs/Vickers hardness of the multilayer antireflective stack ($TiO_2/SiO_2$), $\sigma_{\text{yield}}$ is the compressive fracture limit, $\alpha_i$ is impact angle, and $v_t$ is the threshold chipping velocity ($\approx 4.2\text{ m/s}$ in $6\text{ mbar } CO_2$).

Governing Regime: Low-pressure Knudsen saltation kinetics ($\text{Kn} \approx 0.12$)
2. Interdigitated Capacitive (IDC) Conformal Dielectric Inversion

Thin-film sub-micron comb electrodes embedded directly beneath the sacrificial anti-reflective coating project an evanescent fringing electric field into the top dielectric layer. Utilizing partial capacitance conformal mapping:

$$C_{\text{total}} = 2 \varepsilon_0 L (N-1) \left[ \varepsilon_{\text{sub}} \frac{K(k_{\text{sub}})}{K'(k_{\text{sub}})} + \left(\varepsilon_{\text{arc}}(t) - 1\right) \frac{K(k_{\text{arc}})}{K'(k_{\text{arc}})} \left( 1 - e^{-\frac{2\pi d_{\text{arc}}(t)}{\lambda_{\text{spatial}}}} \right) \right]$$

Where $K(k)$ represents complete elliptic integrals of the first kind, $\lambda_{\text{spatial}}$ is spatial electrode pitch ($15\,\mu\text{m}$), and $d_{\text{arc}}(t)$ is the instantaneous thickness of the optical coating layer.

Resolution Limit: $\Delta d_{\text{arc}} \approx 0.8\text{ nm}$ at $f_{\text{drive}} = 100\text{ kHz}$
3. Davies-Bennett Optical Transmittance & Micro-Pit Scatter

Dielectric thinning shifts the destructive interference quarter-wavelength condition ($\lambda_0 = 4 n_{\text{arc}} d_{\text{arc}}$), while impact micro-pitting introduces RMS surface roughness $\sigma_{\text{rms}}$, reducing direct solar cell photon admittance:

$$T_{\text{spec}}(\lambda, t) = T_0(\lambda) \cdot \exp\left[ - \left( \frac{4\pi \sigma_{\text{rms}}(t) \left(n_{\text{glass}} - 1\right)}{\lambda} \right)^2 \right] \cdot \left[ 1 - R_{\text{Fresnel}}(d_{\text{arc}}(t), \lambda) \right]$$

When $d_{\text{arc}}$ degrades from $120\text{ nm} \to 40\text{ nm}$, reflection losses jump from $1.2\%$ to $8.7\%$, dropping triple-junction InGaP/InGaAs/Ge photovoltaic conversion efficiency by $>18\%$.

AM0 Spectral Integration Range: $350\text{ nm} \le \lambda \le 1800\text{ nm}$
4. Real-Time Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) Erosion Estimator

To distinguish temporary electrostatic dust deposition (reversible) from permanent physical coating ablation (irreversible), the system evaluates the Jacobians of wind speed $v(t)$ and $C(t)$:

$$\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k} = \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k-1} + \mathbf{K}_k \left( \begin{bmatrix} C_{\text{meas}} \\ P_{\text{PV}} \end{bmatrix} - \mathbf{h}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k-1}, v_k) \right), \quad \mathcal{D}_{\text{erosion}} = \oint_{\text{storm}} \nabla_v C(t) \, dt$$

If $\partial C / \partial t < 0$ during sustained periods where $v > v_{\text{saltation}}$, the filter attributes the change to irreversible dielectric stripping, instantly adjusting the mission power allocation model.

Convergence Latency: $< 400\text{ ms}$ on radiation-hardened RAD5545 CPU
